Overview

How I worked this problem, step by step

The task is to design how Plentific gives clients their data in the client's own data warehouse, and the path to get there. This page set shows the order I worked in, and why — starting with who the customer is and what actually exists, before proposing anything.

The one rule I set myself

Understand the problem before designing a solution. The brief is explicit that the client's request — "batch export everything into our warehouse" — is the client's proposed solution, not the problem. If I start from the export, I have already accepted their design. So Steps 1 to 4 contain no solution at all.

The steps

  • Step 1 Who the customers are

    Who pays, who uses, who the data is about. Plain-English questions and answers, the three customer types side by side, and why the person asking for this is not the person who bought Plentific.

  • Step 2 What data exists, and how fast it moves

    What Plentific actually holds, in everyday language, and which customer would want each part. Plus hard evidence on how often it changes — including the fact that it changed on the day I wrote this.

  • Step 3 The jargon, in plain English

    Every technical term I need in the room, explained without jargon: join keys, change capture, access tokens, and why "we only added a field" can still break a client's regulatory return.

  • Step 4 How I frame the problem

    The discipline check: the brief is the specification, the technical research is only evidence. What earns time in a 30 to 40 minute session, and what I deliberately leave out.

What comes next

Steps 5 to 8 are the actual product argument: the problem statement, the options I considered and the one I chose, the sequence of what gets built when, and the commercial and governance commitments. They are deliberately not written yet, because writing them before Steps 1 to 4 would be guessing.

Where the evidence comes from

Three sources, in descending order of authority:

  1. The case study brief itself. This is the specification. Everything I recommend has to answer a question the brief actually asks.
  2. Plentific's public developer documentation at dev.plentific.com — the Client API, the Contractor API, all five published versions, and the full changelog archive.
  3. Public record — Plentific's own site and customer stories, the G-Cloud service listing, and UK regulation (Awaab's Law, and the Regulator of Social Housing's annual data returns).

I have no access to Plentific's internal warehouse, cost base, contracts or roadmap. Where something matters and I cannot know it, I say so and mark it as an assumption to validate rather than quietly filling the gap.

Honesty note on sources

Plentific's own G-Cloud listing says of the public API: "we seek to provide public API endpoints to mirror all functionality available within the platform. Note that this is an ongoing project and we don't currently guarantee parity between the two." So the public documentation is a subset of the real platform, and may lag it. Everything I infer from the API is a floor, not a ceiling — the internal warehouse almost certainly holds more than this. I would want that corrected in the room.
