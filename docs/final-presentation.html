Plentific Data Delivery

Trusted data in the client's warehouse. A stable interface between a product that changes quickly and clients who need pipelines that change slowly.

Opening statement

SLIDE 1

How I'd open the presentation:

"When I read the brief, I don't think the core product question is 'how do we batch export the warehouse?'

I think the question is: how do we let clients build long-lived analytical pipelines against Plentific data without turning every future Plentific product change into a client outage?

That leads me to separate the problem into two products.

First, Plentific needs an internal publication layer that turns fast-changing internal data into governed, tenant-safe, stable external contracts.

Second, on top of that, I would launch a client-facing Data Delivery product.

The biggest mistake would be to interpret this as a file-transfer feature. The product isn't an export. The product is a stable data interface."

"The external schema is an API, even if the transport happens to be a file."

Product A enables Product B. The internal warehouse is an asset, but I would not expose it directly.

Why not expose the internal warehouse?

Because its schema represents Plentific's internal implementation. The moment clients depend on that schema:

Internal refactoring becomes an externally breaking product change.

That directly contradicts the brief's requirement that Plentific should remain able to ship rapidly.

How I fund invisible platform work

I would not ask: "Can we spend six months rebuilding our data platform?"

I would find 2-3 paid design partners, agree the product proposition and price with them, and fund the minimum platform capability required to fulfil those commitments.

That turns: platform investment → enterprise revenue enablement

Rather than: platform investment → internal technical programme

Version and change control

SLIDE 6 — This is the slide I'd spend the most time on

The brief says: "This is the part that quietly decides whether the product survives, and given how often our product changes it is the one we will push hardest on."

My versioning policy

v1 stays v1. If Plentific wants to make a breaking change:

v1 -------------------------+
                           | dual run (both versions active)
v2              -----------+

       client migration period (180 days)
PolicyDecision
Deprecation window180 days for breaking changes
Versions supportedN and N-1 only (current + previous)
Why N/N-1?Indefinite backwards compatibility becomes a tax on every future engineering change. Clients get predictable migration time; Plentific doesn't promise to support every historical schema forever

How this avoids becoming a bureaucracy

Engineering should not have to ask a data committee for permission to change an internal table.

Instead: contract compatibility is automated.

  • CI detects whether source changes affect published contracts
  • PR template checkbox: "Does this change exported schema?" — if yes, requires Data PM review
  • Domain team owns meaning
  • Data platform owns publishing infrastructure
  • Data Product (me) owns the client contract and lifecycle

"I tie schema review to outcomes engineering cares about: fewer 3am pages, fewer angry client calls, fewer 'why did this break?' investigations."

Corrections and late-arriving data

A stable schema doesn't mean the underlying data never changes.

If yesterday we exported work_order_123 status = complete and today an operational correction says status = cancelled:

  • I wouldn't silently mutate yesterday's delivered file
  • The new batch contains a new version of the record
  • Client ingestion is idempotent using: business primary key + updated_at/revision
  • Deletions get explicit tombstones

Each delivery also gets a manifest:

{
  "dataset": "repairs",
  "schema_version": "v1",
  "extract_timestamp": "2026-09-15T02:00:00Z",
  "row_count": 48291,
  "file_count": 1,
  "checksum": "sha256:abc123...",
  "watermark": "2026-09-15T01:45:00Z",
  "status": "complete"
}

This solves the brief's question: "At 3am, whose pipeline is broken?" The manifest lets both sides establish whether Plentific produced the expected batch successfully.

Commercial model

SLIDE 8

The key framing:

"Clients already own or have rights to their data. I am not charging them for ownership of it.

I am charging for a managed product that continuously publishes, secures, versions, documents, monitors and supports that data in a form their warehouse can safely consume."

Pushing back on "nightly full export"

SLIDE 10

The brief asks: "If you think part of that request is the wrong answer to their real problem, say so and tell us what you would offer instead."

What the client says

"Our requirement is a complete export every night."

What I translate that into

What they sayWhat they really need
"Export everything"Access to useful analytical domains
"Every night"Predictable freshness
"Full export"Ability to reconstruct correct current state
"Into my warehouse"Easy, automatable ingestion
"Don't change it"Stable schema contract
"Raw data"Enough granularity to answer questions Plentific hasn't anticipated

My response

"I can meet the underlying requirement that you have a complete and dependable view of your Plentific data every morning.

I wouldn't recommend retransmitting the entire history every night.

We would give you an initial full snapshot and then a versioned incremental feed containing inserts, updates and deletions. You can reconstruct the full current state at any point, but the process is faster, cheaper and significantly easier to recover.

If your current ingestion platform genuinely requires a full snapshot, I'd understand that constraint and explore a transitional full-snapshot option. But I wouldn't make that the architecture for the product."

That answer is strong because I'm pushing back without telling the customer they're wrong.

Expected questions and answers

BACKUP SLIDES / Q&A

Panel challengeMy answer
Why not Snowflake sharing? Fantastic for Snowflake clients, but bad first abstraction for a multi-warehouse customer base. Prove demand first, add it behind the same data contract later
Why not just expose the existing warehouse? Because then internal implementation becomes a public API. Security and schema evolution become coupled to every client. We lose the ability to refactor
Why not use the API? It's transactional, not analytical. 10,000 API calls vs 1 file export. The brief already says it's slow and expensive for both sides
Why Parquet rather than CSV? Typed, compressed, analytical, cheap to process, broadly compatible. CSV/SFTP can remain an exception for legacy clients
What if clients insist on hourly? Make freshness an explicit product tier. Don't make every customer subsidise the operating cost of hourly delivery
What happens when engineering removes a field? Internal field changes freely; published contract doesn't. Translate internally, or release v2 and dual-run with 180-day migration
What if supporting v1 gets expensive? Explicit support lifetime and N/N-1 policy. Compatibility is a promise, not immortality
Who owns schema? Data Product (me) owns external contract and lifecycle. Domain teams own source semantics. Data Platform owns publishing infrastructure
What scares you most? Not cloud compute. Cross-tenant leakage first; uncontrolled bespoke variation second; unbounded backwards compatibility third
What would cause you to stop the project? If discovery shows demand is a handful of bespoke requests with insufficient willingness to pay for a standardised product
Why should clients pay for their own data? They're not paying for ownership. They're paying for the managed interface, delivery, reliability, security and backwards compatibility
What would you validate first? Demand concentration, top datasets, acceptable latency, client warehouse technologies, security expectations, volumes, willingness to pay, current cost of bespoke work
Our eng team hates process. How do you make this stick? Don't frame it as process. Frame it as protection. "This PR check saves you from a 3am page." Tie it to outcomes they care about. Keep it lightweight
What if we tried versioning before and it failed? Usually it's process failure, not tech failure. No enforcement = people bypass it. The fix is tying schema review to outcomes: fewer incidents, fewer support calls
A client wants custom fields. Now what? Three options: (1) If useful to many, add to standard scope. (2) If truly custom, quote separately. (3) If not feasible, say no. We don't expand scope for free

Why I weighted version control highest

  • Least reversible decision
  • Already proven in Plentific's API (dated versions: 2020, 2021, 2023)
  • Touches all four registers (product, engineering, commercial, governance)
  • The question that decides if the product survives

Competitor context

Competitors (Fixflo, Civica, MRI, Yardi) offer manual CSV downloads and transactional APIs. No one offers automated file drops with versioned schemas and SLAs. Plentific already has API versioning — extending it to bulk data is a 6-12 month head start.
